# Example 5: Nominative, Accusative and Dative

## What I asked AI

> Explain the German nominative, accusative and dative cases to me in simple terms. I am learning German at A2 level, so use short explanations and everyday examples. Show me how to work out which case I need instead of only giving me a table to memorise.

## The simple explanation

I find German cases easier when I think about the **job** a person or thing has in the sentence.

### Nominative — who is doing the action?

The nominative is normally the subject.

> **Der Mann** kauft einen Kaffee.

Who is buying? **Der Mann.**

My shortcut: **Who is doing it?**

### Accusative — who or what directly receives the action?

The accusative is often the direct object.

> Der Mann kauft **einen Kaffee**.

What is he buying? **Einen Kaffee.**

Another example:

> Ich sehe **den Mann**.

Who do I see? **Den Mann.**

My shortcut: **Who or what is the action happening to?**

A useful masculine change:

- der → **den**
- ein → **einen**

### Dative — to whom, for whom or with whom?

The dative often identifies the person receiving something.

> Ich gebe **dem Mann** einen Kaffee.

Who is giving? *Ich* → nominative.  
What am I giving? *einen Kaffee* → accusative.  
To whom? **dem Mann** → dative.

Another example:

> Ich spreche **mit dem Mann**.

*Mit* always takes the dative.

My shortcut: **To/for/with whom?**

## All three in one sentence

> **Der Kellner** gibt **dem Gast** **einen Kaffee**.

- **Der Kellner** → nominative: Who is doing it?
- **dem Gast** → dative: To whom?
- **einen Kaffee** → accusative: What is being given?

This was the example that helped me see that cases are not just article changes. They tell me what role each noun has in the sentence.

## Article table

| | Nominative | Accusative | Dative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Masculine | der | den | dem |
| Feminine | die | die | der |
| Neuter | das | das | dem |
| Plural | die | die | den |

For indefinite articles:

| | Nominative | Accusative | Dative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Masculine | ein | einen | einem |
| Feminine | eine | eine | einer |
| Neuter | ein | ein | einem |

## How I use AI to practise this

After the explanation, I can ask AI to create sentences at my level where I have to choose the correct article.

For example:

1. Ich sehe ___ Mann. *(der / den / dem)*
2. Ich spreche mit ___ Frau. *(die / der / den)*
3. ___ Kunde wartet. *(Der / Den / Dem)*
4. Ich gebe ___ Kunden eine Antwort. *(der / den / dem)*

### Answers

1. **den** — accusative
2. **der** — dative because *mit* takes dative
3. **Der** — nominative
4. **dem** — dative

I can then ask AI to explain only the questions I get wrong and generate more exercises on the same case.

## What I learned

For me, the most useful approach is to ask what the noun is doing in the sentence before worrying about the article. **Who is doing it? What is receiving the action? To/for/with whom?**

Using AI this way lets me ask follow-up questions immediately and generate extra practice around the grammar points I find difficult.
