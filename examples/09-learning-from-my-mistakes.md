# Example 9: Learning From My Own Mistakes

## What I asked AI

Instead of asking for another general grammar lesson, I can give AI several sentences I have written and ask it to look for patterns.

> These are mistakes I have made while practising German. Correct them, but also look across all of them and identify the grammar areas I am struggling with most. Explain the patterns simply at A2 level. Then create a short personalised exercise that focuses on those areas.

## Example mistakes

> Ich sehe der Mann.

> Ich spreche mit den Mann.

> Ich möchte ein Termin machen.

> Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich möchte besser sprechen.

> Gestern ich habe Deutsch gelernt.

## AI identifies the patterns

Looking at these sentences together shows several recurring areas to practise.

### 1. Cases and articles

> Ich sehe **den Mann**.

*sehen* has a direct object here, so *der Mann* becomes accusative: **den Mann**.

> Ich spreche mit **dem Mann**.

*mit* always takes the dative: **dem Mann**.

> Ich möchte **einen Termin** vereinbaren.

*Termin* is masculine and is the direct object, so *ein Termin* becomes **einen Termin**.

### 2. Word order with weil

> Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich besser sprechen **möchte**.

In a subordinate clause with *weil*, the conjugated verb goes to the end.

### 3. Word order in Perfekt

> Gestern **habe ich** Deutsch **gelernt**.

When I start with *Gestern*, the conjugated verb still takes the second position. The past participle goes towards the end.

## Personalised mini-practice

Choose or write the correct form.

1. Ich sehe ___ Kunden. *(der / den / dem)*
2. Ich spreche mit ___ Kunden. *(der / den / dem)*
3. Ich brauche ___ Termin. *(ein / einen / einem)*
4. Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich in der Schweiz arbeiten ___.
5. Gestern ___ ich mit meiner Kollegin gesprochen.

### Answers

1. **den**
2. **dem**
3. **einen**
4. **möchte**
5. **habe**

## A useful follow-up prompt

After completing the exercise, I can say:

> I got questions 2 and 4 wrong. Do not give me another full grammar explanation. Give me three new A2 sentences that practise only those two mistakes, and let me answer before you show me the solutions.

This means the next exercise is based on what I actually found difficult rather than repeating things I already understand.

## Why this is useful for me

One mistake on its own does not always tell me what I need to study. Looking at several of my own sentences can reveal that I repeatedly struggle with something specific, such as dative articles or word order.

AI can help me spot that pattern quickly and turn it into targeted practice.

## What I learned about using AI

The quality of the practice improves when I give AI information about **my own mistakes** and tell it what to do with them. Instead of receiving the same general lesson as everyone else, I can use my results from one exercise to decide what I practise next.

For me, this creates a simple learning loop:

**try → make mistakes → identify the pattern → practise the weak area → try again**
